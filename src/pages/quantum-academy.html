Quantum Academy, free lessons in quantum physics and computing

Quantum Academy teaches quantum physics and quantum computing from the ground up. It starts where a first-year course does, with vectors, matrices and the states of one qubit, and builds towards measurement, entanglement and the algorithms that use them.

Seven parts, in reading order

Part 0 is the linear algebra the rest is written in: skip it if you know it, and come back when a lesson links to it. Parts 1 and 6 are context and can be read in any order.

See every lesson

0. Maths foundations · 13 lessons

Complex numbers, vector spaces, inner products, matrices, eigenvalues, tensor products and probability, each with quantum examples.

1. Why quantum? · 3 lessons

Light in quanta, matter waves, atoms and spin: the experiments that forced the theory.

2. The postulates · 9 lessons

Qubits and bra-ket notation, observables, measurement, time evolution, uncertainty and entanglement.

3. Qubits and circuits · 6 lessons

The Bloch sphere, single- and multi-qubit gates, interference and teleportation.

4. Algorithms · 6 lessons

Deutsch-Jozsa, Bernstein-Vazirani, Simon, Grover, the quantum Fourier transform and phase estimation.

5. Wave mechanics · 8 lessons

Wavefunctions, the Schrödinger equation, the particle in a box, tunnelling and the harmonic oscillator.

6. Many particles · 3 lessons

Counting states, spins in a field and the Einstein solid.

Quantum Academy questions

What people ask before they start.

Ask on GitHub

What do I need to know first?

Nothing beyond school maths. Part 0 teaches the complex numbers, linear algebra and probability the rest uses.

Is it free?

Yes. Every lesson is free to read, and the source is MIT licensed.

What is it based on?

University syllabi for quantum and thermal physics and linear algebra, with first-year physics, complex numbers and probability where they supply a prerequisite.

How do I contribute?

Lessons are Markdown files in the repository. Add one to the contents list, put what its code prints in an output block, and CI checks it. Read how to write a lesson
